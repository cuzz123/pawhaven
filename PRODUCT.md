# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Modern pet parents who see their pets as family. They come to PawHaven from multiple mindsets, and the store must serve each:

- **The anxious caregiver** — worries about their pet's wellbeing when they're away. Buying peace of mind through products that work (calming mats, GPS trackers). Needs reassurance, evidence, and clear benefit language.
- **The design-conscious owner** — refuses to let pet products ruin their home aesthetic. Buying beautiful objects that happen to be for pets. Needs visual refinement, lifestyle imagery, and products that look as good as they function.
- **The emotional buyer** — shopping during a meaningful moment: grieving a loss, celebrating a new pet, gifting another pet parent. The purchase is emotional, not utilitarian. Needs warmth, sensitivity, and space to feel.

Secondary: store administrators managing products, orders, and customers through the admin dashboard.

## Product Purpose

PawHaven sells premium smart pet wellness products — calming, safety, feeding, health, travel, and memorial goods — to pet parents who want the best for their animals without compromising their own standards. The store exists to make pet parenting feel less anxious and more connected: products backed by science, designed with love, and sold with integrity.

1% of every purchase is donated to animal shelters.

Success means a customer leaves feeling understood, not sold to — and their pet's life is tangibly better for the purchase.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, Trustworthy, Premium**

Like a well-lit independent pet boutique with natural wood shelving and soft lighting. Curated, not overwhelming. Calm, not clinical. The store feels like a place where the staff know your pet's name and only stock things they'd use for their own.

Voice: confident but gentle. Expert but not lecturing. Emotional but not saccharine. Speaks to pet parents as equals in the shared project of giving animals a good life.

## Anti-references

- **Corporate / clinical**: Vet clinic websites, insurance portals, pharmacy apps. Blue-and-gray, sterile, institutional. Trust through warmth, not authority signaling.
- **Discount / bargain feel**: Bright red sale badges, countdown timers, urgency overlays, crowded grids. PawHaven is premium; it doesn't manufacture scarcity.
- **Overdesigned / flashy**: Excessive animations, gradient text, glassmorphism, design-for-design's-sake. The products and the pets are the heroes, not the CSS.
- **Cutesy / infantilizing**: Baby talk, cartoon paw prints, Comic Sans energy. Pet parents are adults; the bond is real, not childish.

## Design Principles

1. **Warmth over sterility.** Every surface should feel like a well-lit boutique, not a clinic. Natural tones, generous whitespace, human-scale typography. If it feels like a waiting room, redo it.

2. **Trust through clarity.** Premium doesn't mean ornate. Clear product information, honest pricing, straightforward benefits. No dark patterns, fake scarcity, or manipulative urgency.

3. **Honor the bond.** The pet-human relationship is deep and real. Copy, imagery, and interactions should respect that depth. Emotional without being sentimental; sincere without being heavy.

4. **Serve every mindset.** The anxious first-time buyer, the design-conscious regular, and the grieving pet parent all need to feel seen — not sold to. Each surface should hold space for the emotional state the customer arrives with.

5. **Quiet confidence.** Premium doesn't shout. Confident restraint: purposeful typography, intentional spacing, a single accent doing real work. No urgency timers, no blinking "SALE" badges.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target: **WCAG 2.2 AA** compliance
- Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 against background; large text ≥ 3:1
- All interactive elements keyboard-accessible with visible focus indicators
- Screen-reader-friendly: semantic HTML, ARIA labels on icon-only controls, alt text on all product and lifestyle images
- Reduced-motion fallbacks for all animations via `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`
- Color is never the sole indicator of state or information
- Form errors are clear, specific, and helpful — not just red borders
